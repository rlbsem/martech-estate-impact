# ADR 002: Explicit uncertainty and bounded conclusions

Status: accepted and implemented.

A missing export, stale definition, conflicting ownership claim or quiet seasonal job cannot justify a confident retirement decision. Use Supported, Unsupported and Unknown instead of collapsing incomplete evidence into a binary graph.

Separate positive support from completeness of discovery. Admit sources by assertion type, retain conflicting values and permit explicit reviewer resolutions without deleting old claims. Keep proposed architecture separate from observed metadata. Qualify alternatives explicitly; use required event, field and correlation-key containment as a declared contract check, not proof of runtime behavior.

The most favorable assessment is “no identified blockers within reviewed scope.” It is neither a safety guarantee nor deployment authorization. Activity acceptance gates remain independent of architecture feasibility. Witnesses and coverage reasons explain how the conclusion was reached; no invented confidence percentages are used.

Deterministic reasoning is intentional. No LLM or vector search participates in a dependency decision. Future extraction from unstructured documents could propose candidate assertions, but they would enter this same evidence-review process unverified. That future capability is not implemented.

The consequence is more honest unresolved results and more human review work. A narrow scope is useful only when explicit: newly changed outcomes outside it are still disclosed and adverse changes prevent a clean disposition. Human completeness and acceptance declarations must be governed by the adopting organization; this implementation cannot authenticate their truth.
